The short version

  • Predators evolve with nothing telling them to. In 2 of 3 baseline worlds cells get at least 15% of their energy from other cells, and being bitten causes up to 72% of deaths. No cell type, score or genome signal is involved.
  • The same rules can also stay boring. 1 of 3 worlds had almost no predation and were close to one grazing lineage. Which way a world goes looks like chance, so one run proves little.
  • Different ways of life live side by side. One world holds 12% hunters, 14% scavengers, 3% resters, 45% settlers, 27% wanderers, and the maps show they occupy different places.
  • Colonies hold territory. Different genomes keep separate ground with sharp borders, and you can see it directly in the genome-coloured views.
  • Scared hasn't evolved. Cells steer away from bodies in general, but treat bigger and smaller ones about the same, so there's no real fleeing or chasing yet. Hunters mostly sit and bite what's next to them.
  • Scent, gifts and heat are barely used. Each takes under about 1% of spending in baseline, and scent following is weak. Giving cells a feature doesn't mean evolution picks it up.
  • Nothing here beats baseline clearly yet. Several experiments are still waiting for the GPU, so no rule can be called a winner.

These baseline numbers predate a fix to how cell behaviour is measured. Fixed runs are queued and will replace them automatically. Physics isn't affected.

The rules

One kind of cell, one per square. Each has its own recurrent neural network with 672 weights and 8 memory units, and an energy store. Each tick it senses, thinks, and splits its energy across actions. There's no fitness function: energy alone decides who survives, and a cell that fills its womb divides. Nothing here assigns a role.

PhysicsRule
SunGrows ground food. Patchy at several scales, with seasons. Rock ridges form regions joined by gaps.
DeathA dead cell's body and energy become meat, which rots back into ground food.
BitingPulls energy from the cell in front, harder for stronger cells. Only open sides can be reached, so being inside a clump protects you. It loses energy: biters keep half and the rest spills as meat.
HeatLands on the four squares around the emitter and burns whoever is there.
EnzymeLets anyone nearby eat ground food up to 3x faster, so cheating is possible.
ScentFour channels, from long trails to short shouts, with no built-in meaning. A cell only emits what its brain chooses.
Touch signalsTwo numbers a cell chooses to show its neighbours.
SightBodies up to 4 squares away in 8 directions: how close, bigger or smaller, and what signals they show.
HearingMoving makes noise, louder for heavier bodies, so stillness hides you.
SharingTwo touching cells that both agree level out their energy.
BirthA clone, or a mix with any willing neighbour. Whole memory units come from one parent or the other. Mutations change about 10% of weights.
CommitmentSpending a little on something barely works, so dabbling is wasteful.
RainA random cell lands now and then, so the world can't die for good.

Removed because they decide for the cells: an odour derived from the genome, a built-in sense of how related a neighbour is, and a genome check on who can mate. Cells must work out who is who from what others choose to show. Colour by genome is only for us watching: nearby colours mean similar weights, using the two or three main axes along which the population's genomes differ.

Ways of living

Every cell is the same kind of thing. To read the results I sort them by what they do. These labels are mine, cells never see them, and the cut-offs are simple:

  • Hunters: at least 40% of energy from other cells, more from biting than from meat.
  • Scavengers: the same 40%, more from meat than from biting.
  • Resters: spend at least half their budget resting and barely move.
  • Settlers: mostly graze and move under 10% of the time.
  • Wanderers: mostly graze and move more than that.

Cells under age 50 aren't counted, because they haven't done enough to label.

What they actually rely on

A cell using something doesn't prove it matters. So each evolved world is copied, one ability is switched off, and the population is compared with an untouched copy over the next 30,000 ticks.

Queued for the GPU, and will appear here when it runs.

Species

Species here means genome groups: cells joined if their weights are close, with no rule creating them. Genomes drift fast, about 0.03 of weight difference per generation, so a low threshold only finds recent family. That's why 0.4 is the reference.

How worlds change over time

Same rules and same settings, with only the random seed different. These worlds don't converge on one outcome.

How big it goes

The CPU version reached 15.305k cells at about 67 ticks per second on 4 threads. The GPU version is the same idea on a 7900 XT that also draws the desktop. Work goes to the card in short pieces, and each run takes turns with other GPU jobs.

The world's physics isn't exactly the same at every size. A 512² world has about 4 season regions, and 4096² has 256, so a big world always has somewhere in good season. And 8192² is a warning: the memory limit at the time forced the sun down 80% so 2.6 million cells would fit, and cells ended up with 0.6 neighbours on average. Predators vanished. Size alone doesn't make a world richer: density matters too.

Limits

  • Few runs. Three baseline seeds, and two or three for most experiments. Worlds differ a lot by chance, so treat everything here as leads.
  • Giant worlds are young. The 4096² world ran about 16,000 ticks from copied tiles, so the regular pattern you can see comes from that. It isn't long enough for much new evolution.
  • 512² isn't the same physics as 4096². Small worlds have fewer season regions and smaller landscape features, so what's true at one size may not carry over.
  • Labels are mine. Hunter, settler and the rest are cut-offs for reading results, and different cut-offs would shift the shares.
  • Species depend on a threshold. The chart shows how much. Some worlds hold steady across thresholds, and others don't.
  • The GPU is shared. Experiments wait behind other projects, so this page updates slowly.
  • Checked: 13 tests cover the energy ledger, grid consistency, repeatable runs and each rule against plain arithmetic. An independent review of the engine found no concurrency or energy bugs. It did find measurement problems, which are fixed, and the population limit that shaped the 4096² and 8192² runs.
