32 worlds ran on plain physics and 22 sets of 16 on mixes of extra rules. A strategy is a group of cells that behave alike, and it lasts if it holds at least 3% of cells for three samples in a row. Plain worlds keep a median of 2. Seven of the mixes died out completely, and most of the others got duller. One mix, b0-0011, scores 3, but with a median of 1,146 cells nearly all one species on the same diet.

Biters don't choose whom they bite. Across 205 worlds, the bite energy taken from close kin matches how many close kin are next to the biter (correlation 0.96).

More time doesn't help. 16 plain worlds run five times longer end with a median of 2 strategies over 3%, the same as before, and 3.4 behaviour dimensions, also the same.

Bodies are physically impossible in this engine. One cell per square, no bonds, and force can't cross a group. So a new engine is being built.

Population over time is not a cycle

Cells in long-b0-0001-t000 swing between about 6,400 and 13,300 with a period of 4 samples, which looks like a population cycle. It isn't one. The world has an 8,000-tick year, and samples come every 30,000 ticks, which is 3.75 years. So each sample lands a quarter of a year earlier in the season than the last, and the same point comes round every 4 samples. Sort the samples by point in the year and the cycle collapses into seasons.

After removing each world's slow trend, the point in the year explains at least 75% of the swing in 12 of 16 plain worlds. The rest are t013 at 22%, t004 at 36%, t011 at 58%, t006 at 67%. Worlds peak at different points in the year. Both ends of the comparison above, 120,000 and 600,000 ticks, fall at the same point in the year, so the before and after isn't affected.

Things that looked real and weren't

  • Population cycles. long-b0-0001-t000 and others seem to swing, about 6,400 to 13,300 cells, with a period of 4 samples. Samples every 30,000 ticks alias against the built-in 8,000-tick year: it's seasons. Shown above. Lesson: don't sample at a period that shares a low common multiple with the season.
  • Life hugging the outer wall in mineral worlds. Real, but an artefact of the mineral rule. Mineral piles against the outer rock at a median of 11 to 35 times the density inside the world, set by set across all the flow settings tried, and cells follow it. Next to natural rock it's about 7 to 11 times higher too, in the two worlds measured. So mineral worlds didn't test a second resource. It's likely carried there by cells and released where they die, but that mechanism isn't tested.
  • "Traffic on lines" as a sign of trails. No map showed lines, so it's useless as a trail detector as built. It doesn't even track colonies: in plain worlds its rank correlation with colony count is +0.01. The 38 live worlds with no colonies have a median of 87, against 87 for all live worlds. Readers saw it rise wherever cells pace around, whether inside a colony or along a wall.
  • "Lives on other cells: 27%" in our earlier rank tables is the average of per-cell ratios, in b0-0000-t001. The world's energy flows say 8% for the same world. Flows are what this page uses.

Why this can't make bodies, and what's next

This engine allows one cell per square. Cells have no bond between them, and force doesn't cross a group: the most a cell can do is shove one neighbour. So there is no way for a group to hold together, move together or push. No choice of rule in this engine can change that, so running more of it won't produce bodies.

A new engine is being designed: soft discs in continuous space that can stick to each other, pull, push, and trade energy across contact. It keeps the same discipline. One kind of cell, no fitness function, no body IDs, energy conserved, and an off switch on every rule so each can be tested against a control. The bar is bodies that reproduce as units with no designed help.

Read the design. It works out supply and demand before anything is built.
