# Fast Conway Life: evidence and implementation directions
Research date: 2026-10-31. Research only; no benchmarks were run locally.

## Interpretation
CUPS = grid sites × generations / elapsed seconds, including dead sites for dense solvers. A trillion updates/s is **not** a trillion-cell resident board. HashLife can skip repeated work and generations; its effective CUPS cannot be compared directly with dense random-board CUPS. These are the strongest results located, not proof of an absolute world record.

## Techniques and measured gains
- **Packed SWAR/bit slicing:** align eight neighbor words, then use XOR/AND/OR half/full adders to count independent bit lanes without inter-cell carries. Test count=3 or (alive and count=2). This is parallel computation, not merely packing followed by bit extraction. [Fujita journal manuscript](https://www.cs.hiroshima-u.ac.jp/cs/_media/life-ijfcs.pdf).
- **Layouts:** byte cells permit simple SIMD additions; packed bits cut state storage 8× versus bytes, 32× versus u32 cells (arithmetic). Nibbles simplify independent addition. Binary Banter's i7-6700K rates imply 18× nibble/trivial, 1.39× bit/nibble, 3.96× SIMD/bit, and 2.55× threaded/SIMD. These gains are cumulative implementation comparisons, not universal factors. [Results](https://binary-banter.github.io/game-of-life/).
- **Temporal blocking:** compute multiple generations in registers/shared memory before writing global state. A T-generation tile needs a T-cell halo; redundant halo computation trades against bandwidth and dispatch overhead. Fujita combines eight steps with word reuse and shuffles; its overall reported >68× improvement over earlier work does **not** isolate temporal blocking's gain. [2015 conclusion](https://tk-www.elcom.nitech.ac.jp/WTCS2015/WTCS2015_Proceedings.pdf).
- **CUDA versus portable kernels:** NVIDIA ternary boolean operations and warp exchange can improve packed kernels; they are architecture-specific. Binary Banter's A40 CUDA/OpenCL ratio is 1.46× overall, not an isolated instruction gain. [Results](https://binary-banter.github.io/game-of-life/).
- **Configuration evidence:** current [settings.toml](https://github.com/binary-banter/fast-game-of-life/blob/master/settings.toml) has CUDA 32-thread groups, 16 rows/thread, 16 steps; OpenCL 256-thread groups, three rows/thread, vertical padding 16. Treat tuning as hardware-dependent.
- **GPU vectorization:** QuanTaichi's 546.0 → 3.4 ms is ≈161×. CPU 9313.4 → 417.2 ms is ≈22×. Its unvectorized quantized baseline suffers excessive atomic read-modify-write, so this is not evidence that packing alone beats a good byte implementation by 161×. [Table 3 and discussion](https://yuanming.taichi.graphics/publication/2021-quantaichi/quantaichi.pdf).
- **Active tiles / sparse tracking:** retain occupied/changing tiles plus neighbor halos; use per-tile bit kernels. Empty tiles are safe to omit only when neighboring activity cannot reach them during the skipped interval. Static and periodic regions need suitable change/phase bookkeeping. Expected gain depends on active fraction and queue overhead; no portable measured factor located. Golly identifies QuickLife as conventional/non-hashing, and recommends it over HashLife for chaos. [QuickLife](https://golly.sourceforge.io/Help/Algorithms/QuickLife.html), [HashLife](https://golly.sourceforge.io/Help/Algorithms/HashLife.html).
- **HashLife:** canonicalized quadtrees plus memoized evolution reuse identical regions and jump over generations. Gosper invented it; Rokicki's 2006 exposition demonstrates trillions of generations and billions of cells. Huge benefit for repetition, potentially poor for random chaos; there is no meaningful fixed speedup. [Rokicki article mirror](https://github.com/mafm/HashLife), [Golly behavior](https://golly.sourceforge.io/Help/Algorithms/HashLife.html).
- **QuickLife / Golly:** a serious reference for sparse conventional stepping, not synonymous with HashLife. QuanTaichi's particular random-board QuickLife result is ≈1.31× faster than its CPU packed brute force; pattern dependence dominates comparisons. [Golly docs](https://golly.sourceforge.io/Help/Algorithms/QuickLife.html), [paper](https://yuanming.taichi.graphics/publication/2021-quantaichi/quantaichi.pdf).
- **Lookup tables:** small neighborhood/block mappings replace repeated logic, trading table indexing and cache pressure against Boolean arithmetic. Fiser reports up to 45× CPU speedup in the conclusion (60× in the introduction), with a different winning GPU algorithm. Baselines differ; do not present either as an isolated LUT gain. [Article](https://marekfiser.com/projects/conways-game-of-life-on-gpu-using-cuda/).
- **lifelib / apgsearch:** optimized rule-generated C/assembly supports AVX-512. The 2018 change reports AVX2 soup search 3875→4025 soups/s (+4%), AVX-512 3600→5040 (+40%); SKUs absent. Soups/s includes search/classification and cannot honestly become CUPS without trajectories. [Code-generation docs](https://gitlab.com/apgoucher/lifelib/blob/master/doc/exampleusage.md), [optimization change](https://gitlab.com/apgoucher/lifelib/-/merge_requests/4).
- **Modern Rust reference:** gol_engines implements parallel HashLife and StreamLife, with bounded hash tables and GC. Its examples include 4096 generations in 13.8 s at 12 GiB versus 37.5 s at 6 GiB for a metaglider; hardware unspecified, therefore not a CUPS record. [README/source](https://github.com/das67333/gol_engines).
- **LifeHash ambiguity:** the identified LifeHash project makes deterministic visual identifiers from SHA-256-seeded 16×16 Life; it is not a high-performance simulation algorithm. No credible separate “LifeHash” engine benchmark located; likely confusion with HashLife. [Developer documentation](https://developer.blockchaincommons.com/lifehash/).

## Million, billion, trillion sites
The following are **derived storage requirements**, not measured throughput. Two dense buffers, one bit/site, excluding halos, queues, alignment, rendering, and other VRAM users; representation basis: [Fujita](https://www.cs.hiroshima-u.ac.jp/cs/_media/life-ijfcs.pdf).

| Grid sites | Packed two buffers | Byte-cell two buffers | Implication for 20 GB GPU |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 10^6 | 0.25 MB | 2 MB | Fits; dispatch/host/render overhead can dominate |
| 10^9 | 250 MB | 2 GB | Fits packed comfortably; benchmark full resident sweep |
| 10^12 | 250 GB | 2 TB | Dense residency impossible on this GPU |

- **Dense beyond VRAM:** Fujita §8 partitions the universe, loads extended subuniverses from SSD through host RAM, evolves several generations on GPU, and writes clean interiors back. Halos grow with batched steps, amortizing transfers at the cost of redundant work. This is a published out-of-core method; no verified trillion-site timing extracted in this research. [Journal manuscript, §§8–9](https://www.cs.hiroshima-u.ac.jp/cs/_media/life-ijfcs.pdf).
- **Huge structured universes:** compressed DAGs can represent vastly more than 10^12 sites without dense allocation. gol_engines' recursive-metafication example prints a population far above 10^12; this demonstrates representation, not dense random evolution at that size. [Example and source](https://github.com/das67333/gol_engines#metafy).
- **Sparse large physical grids:** QuanTaichi demonstrates a 262144² domain (~68.7 billion sites), ~20.55 billion active sites, with quantized sparse storage. It does not demonstrate a dense trillion-site board. [Table 2](https://yuanming.taichi.graphics/publication/2021-quantaichi/quantaichi.pdf).
- **Trillion-site test inputs exist:** Girgin's 2023 dataset contains 1000000² grids at very low live fractions. This establishes dataset size, not simulation throughput or resident dense execution. [Zenodo dataset](https://zenodo.org/records/10011942).
- **Distributed evidence:** Catagolue/apgsearch had collectively explored 210 trillion separate 16×16 soups by January 2021; those independent searches are not one coupled trillion-site universe. [Goucher's report](https://cp4space.hatsya.com/2021/01/31/assorted-topics/).
- **Engineering inference:** a coupled dense trillion-site run could partition packed tiles across nodes/GPUs and exchange halos, with multi-generation batches. Capacity alone needs at least 13×20 GB GPUs for 250 GB state, before overhead; network and boundary costs determine speed. No credible measured 10^12-site MPI/GPU CUPS record was located. Do not claim one.

## Consequences for Rust + wgpu on RX 7900 XT / 7800X3D
1. Prototype packed u32 bit-parallel Boolean kernels and multi-generation tiles, with a simple byte/u32 baseline for correctness. This follows the strongest dense results above; **8.939 TCUPS on XTX is a comparison target, not an XT prediction**. [AMD evidence](https://binary-banter.github.io/game-of-life/).
2. Use a CPU packed SIMD implementation and benchmark AVX2 versus AVX-512 locally; no verified 7800X3D result was found. lifelib's AVX-512 advantage is workload-specific. [lifelib evidence](https://gitlab.com/apgoucher/lifelib/-/merge_requests/4).
3. Handle billion-site worlds with tiled buffers; inspect actual adapter/device limits and request supported limits. Buffer size, binding size, dispatch counts, and workgroup storage are distinct constraints; a single giant texture is unnecessary. [wgpu Limits](https://docs.rs/wgpu/latest/wgpu/struct.Limits.html).
4. For trillion-site scope, explicitly choose sparse/HashLife semantics or an out-of-core/distributed dense solver. Publish density, topology, generations/batch, useful updates excluding halo recomputation, GPU timing and end-to-end timing, and correctness checks. Without these, “fastest” comparisons remain inconclusive.
